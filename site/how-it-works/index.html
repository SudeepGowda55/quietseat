From a voice in the call to a line on your glasses.

Three parts, all on hardware you already carry: a Discord bot and two small models on your laptop, and an app on the G2 that only draws screens and sends taps back.

Replay of a recorded calldevelopment data

Consent, per person, per call.

When you join a voice channel, the bot follows you in with its microphone muted and posts a card in the call's chat. Pressing Caption me opts you in; No thanks / stop opts you out at any time, and leaving the call ends your consent. When you leave, the bot leaves too and deletes the card.

For people who have not opted in, the bot never subscribes to their audio. It only notices that someone is talking, so the glasses can say “Sam speaking (not captioned)” and you know to look up.

Speech to text on your laptop.

Discord sends each person's voice separately. After 700 ms of silence the bot turns that turn into a 16 kHz WAV and sends it to a whisper.cpp server running small.en on 127.0.0.1. The request carries your names as a prompt, so whisper spells them closer to right.

  • Clips under 0.4 s are skipped, and whisper's habit of hearing “Thank you.” in silence is filtered out.
  • Turns are transcribed one at a time, so captions stay in the order people spoke.
  • The caption reaches the glasses about 0.5 s after a sentence ends, on an M1 MacBook Air with 8 GB.

Captions screen: the channel, how many are captioned, then the newest lines that fit

Asked by name, not talked about.

A line switches the glasses to the question only when all three rules hold. Try it: this box runs the bot's own rules from bot/src/names.js, with the default names Sudeep, Sudhi and Sudip.

    Your name, loosely

    One word matches one of your names exactly, or within one letter for names of five letters or more. Whisper's “Sudhi” and “Sudip” both reach Sudeep; “Sudeepa” is someone else.

    A question, strictly

    It ends in a question mark, or has a helper verb and its subject (“can you”, “are we”), or a clause starts with a question word.

    Said to you

    It says “you”, or your name stands alone between commas, the way people call someone. “Did Sudeep push the fix?” is about you, so it stays a caption.

    After a reply, new questions wait 20 s, and only one question is on screen at a time.

    Three replies, checked before you see them.

    A 1.5B language model on the laptop (llama.cpp) gets the question and the two lines before it, and must answer within 2.5 s as JSON with three fields:

    • accept: yes, or a neutral answer.
    • decline: no or not now, politely.
    • ask: one short question back, ending in “?”.

    Small models make things up, so each reply is checked. A reply that brings in a name in quotes, a number, a time or a branch name the question did not have is swapped for a built-in reply. So is a decline that says yes, or an ask that does not ask. If the model is slow or not running, the built-in replies are used, so the glasses never wait. Replies are cut to 60 characters to fit the screen.

    Every screen the G2 shows.

    The glasses app is a thin client: it connects to the bot on your laptop over WebSocket with a pairing token, draws what it is sent at most every 120 ms, and sends back taps from the R1 ring. Text is plain ASCII, because the glasses drop anything else.
